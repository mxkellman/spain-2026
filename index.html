Part one · three nights

Barcelona

Short days — the sun is gone by twenty-five past five — so the outdoor and hilltop things go in the morning and the houses that stay open late take the evenings. Monday is the awkward one: most museums shut. That is exactly why it becomes the making day, with chocolate in the morning and ceramic after lunch.

Three nights · 21–24 November

Where to stay in Barcelona

Five options, cheapest first, and every price below includes breakfast for all three of you — booked on the hotel's breakfast-inclusive rate rather than the headline room-only one. Each also has a proper double bed plus a separate bed or pullout for her, confirmed on the live booking page. Three nights, taxes and the city tourist tax in.

Part two · three nights

Madrid

You arrive off the train at two on Tuesday with the Christmas lights already up, give Wednesday to the Royal Palace and the galleries under it, and have the whole of Thursday free to spend however you like. Friday is one museum and the airport.

Three nights · 24–27 November

Where to stay in Madrid

Harder than Barcelona: you are booking straight through a trade fair — Genera + Matelec at IFEMA, 24 to 26 November — and Madrid's four-star stock is unusually committed to twins and bunk beds. Five options, cheapest first, all with breakfast included, one deliberately over the ceiling. Comparable hotels a week later are 23–39% cheaper.

The practical layer

Money, trains and small print

Barcelona charges a tourist tax. Madrid doesn't.

A Barcelona four-star is €8.40 per person per night. Guests sixteen and under are exempt, so she pays nothing: two adults × three nights = €54, settled at checkout.

Madrid has no city tourist tax. Spanish rates quoted to consumers already include the 10% IVA, so there is no second surprise at the desk.

The train is three hours, not two and a half

Speed restrictions on the Madrid–Barcelona line run through 13 December 2026. Real journeys are 3h02 at best, 3h15 typical. Thirty-four trains a day, first at 05:40, last at 20:20.

Renfe gives under-14s 40% off. Ouigo charges a flat €7 for ages 4–13 but €5 per extra bag — with three transatlantic suitcases, price it fully before assuming it is cheaper.

She pays full fare on the metro

Only under-4s ride free in Barcelona. Buy 3 × T-casual (€13.00 each, ten journeys) or 4 × T-familiar (€11.50, eight shared journeys). Either lands around €40–46 for the stay.

Taxis are worth it in three situations: the airport with luggage, coming down from Park Güell with a tired child, and any evening that has run long. A cross-city fare is €7–15.

Madrid airport: the €33 rule

A taxi between Barajas and anywhere inside the M-30 is a flat €33, around the clock, no supplements. All the Madrid hotels here qualify. The 203 bus from Atocha is €5 each and takes forty minutes to T4.

Allow three hours before a transatlantic departure from T4 or T4S; two and a half from T1. Check your terminal in the airline app the night before.

Dinner at nine, and how to cope

Spanish kitchens open for dinner at 20:00–20:30 and 21:00 is normal. Make lunch the big meal and take the €13–20 menú del día, and bridge the gap with a merienda of churros and thick chocolate around half five — a real meal slot here, not a cheat.

The trick that actually works with a child is eat at a counter, not a table. Barcelona's Eixample tapas houses — Cervecería Catalana, Vinitus, Ciudad Condal, La Bodegueta — serve straight through the afternoon, so at 20:00 you get instant service in an empty room, while the same place at 21:15 is a forty-minute wait. In Madrid the equivalents are Casa Labra, which starts dinner at 18:00, La Barraca at 19:30, and Lhardy's continuous kitchen.

Each hotel above lists three places within a few minutes' walk, checked for the nights you are actually there — which matters in Barcelona, where a lot of good restaurants close on Sunday or Monday.

Why every rate here includes breakfast

Spanish hotels sell the same room at three prices — room only, breakfast included, and half board — and the number you see first is almost always the room-only one. Every figure in this brochure is the breakfast-inclusive rate for the right room, so the comparison is like for like.

It is usually worth it. Catalonia Plaza Catalunya charges €29 a head for breakfast bought separately, so for three people the inclusive rate nearly pays for itself. Two properties dropped off the list entirely on this rule: an apart-hotel with no meal service, and one whose breakfast rate pushed a small room over your ceiling.

Book the scarce things now

Sagrada Família tower slots and Park Güell entries genuinely sell out. The chocolate workshop has no published schedule at all. Hotel rooms that sleep three are down to one left at two of the Madrid options. Everything recommended here has free cancellation — hold it now and re-shop in October.
